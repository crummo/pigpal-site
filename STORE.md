# App Store metadata — and how not to look like a payments app

Pig Pal shows dollar amounts, balances and "earned" totals. It holds no money, moves no money,
touches no bank and issues no card — a balance is a note of what a parent has decided they owe a
child. Everything below exists to keep a reviewer from mistaking the first sentence for the
second, because financial-services apps are reviewed against a different and much slower bar.

## The listing

**Category: Education.** Second choice Lifestyle. **Never Finance** — that category is where
banking and money-transfer apps live, it invites 3.2.1(viii) scrutiny about whether the submitter
is a registered financial institution, and Pig Pal gains nothing from being there.

**Subtitle (30 chars):** `Chores, allowance & goals`

**Promotional text / first lines of the description.** The first two sentences are what a reviewer
reads. Lead with chores and responsibility, and put the disclaimer high rather than burying it:

> Pig Pal turns the chore chart on the fridge into something a kid can actually see. Tick a chore
> off, a parent approves, and the balance goes up.
>
> Pig Pal keeps score — it doesn't hold or move money, and it isn't connected to a bank or a card.
> A balance is a note of what a grown-up has decided they owe. Real money changes hands the way it
> always did.

**Keywords to use:** chores, allowance, pocket money, chore chart, kids, family, savings goals,
responsibility, reward chart.

**Keywords and phrases to avoid entirely**, in the name, subtitle, keywords, description and
screenshots: bank, banking, debit, card, wallet, transfer, send money, payments, cash out,
withdraw, deposit, account (in the financial sense), fintech, invest.

**Screenshot captions** should describe the behaviour, not the money: "Chores a kid can tick off",
"Approve from your phone", not "Send your kid $5".

## The two questions that decide the review lane

**"Is this app made for kids?" — answer no.** Pig Pal's paying user and administrator is a parent;
purchases sit behind the parent PIN. Answering yes puts you in the Kids Category, which bans
third-party analytics and advertising outright and requires a parental gate in front of every
purchase opportunity. You'd gain nothing and inherit the rules.

**Age rating: 4+.** Nothing in the app warrants more.

## What already says it in the product

- **In the app:** the About screen states it under the mission card.
- **On the site:** a "Not a bank" section, above the privacy one.
- **In the terms:** `terms.html` says it twice, including "It is not a bank and it does not hold,
  move or transfer money."

If any future feature genuinely moves money — a real card, a bank link, person-to-person transfer
— every line above stops being true and the whole submission changes lane. That's a business
decision long before it's an engineering one.
